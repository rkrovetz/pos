# Annotation Notes

In the assessment, I evaluated 15 words that could be adjectives, and I broke down the words into three groups: NN/JJ, RB/JJ, and VBG/JJ (ambiguity with nouns, adverbs, and verb forms ending in "ing").  I compared the tagging of these words using a small language model  against the tagging using a transformer model.  The comparison assessed ten cases where the taggers agreed and where they disagreed for each of the two tags.  As mentioned in the paper, in some cases I did not have ten cases for each word+pos and both conditions.

In the paper I mis-wrote one of the examples.  I found that hyphenated contexts can cause tagging problems, and I used "cold-hearted", "real-valued", and "short-lived" as examples where adjectives were incorrectly tagged as adverbs.  The example with "cold" should have been "cold-hardiest", and this appears in the provided sample.   In reviewing "real-valued", I found that the tagging of "real" varies depending on context.  For "short-lived", all of the examples I reviewed were incorrectly tagged.

I did some clean up of the Wikipedia output.  I revised the results so that each sentence from the Wikipedia has at least three words and no more than seventy words.  There were some "sentences" in the Wikipedia that were over 9,000 words long; these were lists rather than sentences.  I also replaced HTML entities with the character that they correspond to.  The dataset was processed and stored using UTF-8 encoding (Unicode).  In reviewing the output, there is more cleaning that is possible (e.g., removing URLs), but it isn't clear to me that the remaining noise is a significant impediment to creating improved taggers.  If some of the contexts do create impediments they can be filtered out.

I made a second assessment using the cleaned version of the Wikipedia, and that is what I am providing so that people can replicate the results.  The results were very similar to the first assessment; the average accuracy for cases where the taggers agree is higher than where they disagree, and the accuracy rates are 54% to 82%, which is in accord with the first assessment.

In the paper I said that tagging ambiguity between an adverb and adjective (RB/JJ), and tagging ambiguity between a verb form and an adjective (VBG/JJ) is harder than noun and adjective ambiguity.  That is based not only on the accuracy results, but also my experience.  There were a few tokens where I had difficulty in judging whether the tagging was correct.  These were for the words _binding_ and _standing_.  How should we tag contexts like _antigen binding sites_?   Syntactically, _binding_ is an adjective, but semantically it is acting like a verb.  Sometimes the tagger would tag it as VBG, and I labeled my judgment for such cases as "UNSURE".  I prefer a semantic tag, and I graded such cases as correct in my assessment.  Accuracy would be even lower if I did not do so.  There were also cases like _standing ovation_, which is different from a _standing agreement_.  The word _standing_ in _standing ovation_ is an adjective, but semantically it is similar to the verb.  I graded the adjective tag as correct because it is a modification of _ovation_.  I mention such cases in order to explain some of the difficulties that were encountered.

The paper mentions that incorrect agreement on tags between the small language language model and the transformer was responsible for not having a full set of 600 tokens to assess.  In looking at the problem further, the distribution of part of speech is also a factor.  The word _cold_ is used much less often as an adverb than as an adjective or a noun.  I found a few more examples in the data where there was common agreement on the wrong tag for this word, but the skew in distribution is just as much an issue.

Finally, I want to clarify that the test has the word _binding_  as an adjective, but not as a verb form (VBG).  In the test, the ambiguity is between a noun and an adjective.  I included it as a verb in the assessment set because ambiguity between VBG and JJ is one of the most common reasons for mistakes in tagging.
